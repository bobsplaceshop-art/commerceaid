# Stage 008E.2 - Guest Order Claim

**Date opened:** 2026-09-28
**Status: Complete (2026-09-28)**

(customer auth, complete), Stage 008E.1 (settings

---

## Purpose

Reconcile guest orders with existing customer accounts after
the fact, without adding friction at checkout.

The problem: an existing customer places a guest order using
their account email. The order is created with customer_id
NULL. The customer later logs in and asks "why isn't my order
in my history?"

Industry patterns:

  - WooCommerce and BigCommerce (default): the guest order
    stays orphan. Customer must contact support.
  - BigCommerce (optional): force login at checkout for
    existing emails. Adds friction.
  - Shopify: no native reconciliation.

The design here: move the linking decision from checkout (the
worst moment to add friction) to the next login (the natural
moment to manage an account).

---

## Scope

**In scope:**

- Migration: claimable_by_customer_id and claimed_at columns
  on commerce_orders.
- Toggle in Store Settings -> Compliance tab, off by default,
  with a legal disclaimer on enable.
- CheckoutService populates claimable_by_customer_id when a
  guest order's email matches an existing customer AND the
  toggle is on.
- Notification on the customer dashboard when the customer
  has claimable orders.
- Claim page at /account/claims with Link all and Not now
  actions.
- OrderRepository::listClaimableForCustomer() and
  claimOrders().

**Out of scope (deferred):**

- Retroactive scanning of existing guest orders. The toggle
  affects new orders only. Merchants who enable it want future
  reconciliation, not archaeology.
- Admin un-claim action. A future stage can add a badge and a
  reverse action if fraud reports warrant it.
- Per-order selective claim (Link this one, not that one).
  Link all or Not now is enough for the first pass.
- Email notification when a claim is available. The
  dashboard notification is enough.

## The Toggle

Setting key: `compliance.guest_claim.enabled`
Default: false

Location: Store Settings -> Compliance tab

Why off by default:

  The feature's value depends on the merchant's customer mix.
  A B2C shop with mostly one-time buyers gets little benefit
  and takes on the typo risk (a guest mistypes an existing
  customer's email, the wrong person sees the claim offer).
  A B2B or repeat-purchase shop gets a lot of value. Off by
  default lets each merchant decide.

Disclaimer shown when the admin enables the setting:

  "When enabled, guest orders placed with an email that
   matches an existing customer account will be marked as
   claimable by that customer. The customer will be offered
   a link to claim the order on their next login.

   Legal note (DSGVO): the claimable link asserts that the
   entered email matched an account email. It does not
   assert that the customer owns the order. Ownership is
   established only when the customer explicitly claims.

   Operational note: if a guest mistypes an existing
   customer's email, the wrong customer may be offered the
   claim. Consider whether the reconciliation benefit
   outweighs this risk for your business."

The disclaimer is the `hint` field of the setting definition.
The admin sees it under the toggle in the Compliance tab.
No modal, no gate - just an honest description of what the
feature does and the tradeoff.

## Schema

Migration 0305_add_order_claim_columns.sql:

  ALTER TABLE commerce_orders
    ADD COLUMN claimable_by_customer_id BIGINT UNSIGNED NULL
        AFTER customer_id,
    ADD COLUMN claimed_at DATETIME NULL
        AFTER claimable_by_customer_id,
    ADD KEY idx_com_order_claimable
        (claimable_by_customer_id, claimed_at),
    ADD CONSTRAINT fk_com_order_claimable
        FOREIGN KEY (claimable_by_customer_id)
        REFERENCES commerce_customers (id)
        ON DELETE SET NULL;

Three states the columns support:

  customer_id NULL, claimable NULL, claimed NULL
    Pure guest. No email match at order time, or the toggle
    was off.

  customer_id NULL, claimable = X, claimed NULL
    Guest order with an email matching customer X. Awaiting
    a claim decision.

  customer_id = X, claimable NULL, claimed = timestamp
    Claimed by customer X.

  customer_id = X, claimable NULL, claimed NULL
    Normal customer order (placed while logged in).

The FK is ON DELETE SET NULL so a customer deletion clears
the pending claim rather than orphaning the column.

The column name is claimable_by_customer_id, not
customer_id or belongs_to_customer_id. The distinction is
deliberate: the column records a tentative match, not
asserted ownership. The DSGVO framing depends on this.

## The Checkout Hook

CheckoutService::placeOrder(), before the order header INSERT:

  if the toggle is on AND the order is a guest order
  (customer is null) AND the email matches an existing
  customer:

    $match = $customerRepository->findByEmail($email);
    if ($match !== null) {
        $claimableBy = $match->id;
    }

The value goes into the INSERT as 'claimable_by_customer_id'.

Cost: one extra SELECT per guest order. Only runs when the
toggle is on.

The match is case-insensitive (findByEmail already lowercases
and trims). The order's guest_email column stores the email
as entered by the customer.

## The Customer Side

### Notification

On /account, if the customer has any claimable orders:

  A banner appears above the fold:
    "We noticed N orders placed with your email address
     before you had an account. Would you like to link them
     to your account?"
    [Review orders]   [Not now]

The "Review orders" button navigates to /account/claims.
The "Not now" button dismisses the notification for this
session (a cookie or a flash, not a permanent setting).
The notification reappears on the next login if the
customer has not decided.

### Claim page

Route: GET /account/claims

A table listing the claimable orders:

  Order number | Date | Total | Items | Status

An "About this" notice above the table explains:

  "These orders were placed with your email address before
   you logged in or created an account. If they are yours,
   you can link them to your account. If they are not yours,
   you can leave them as guest orders - they will not appear
   in your history."

Two actions:
  [Link all to my account]      (POST)
  [Leave as guest orders]        (POST, dismisses)

The page links to each order's confirmation URL so the
customer can inspect before linking. The confirmation URL
is the only way to view a guest order - it holds the order
number.

### Claim action

POST /account/claims/link

  For each claimable order for this customer:
    UPDATE commerce_orders
       SET customer_id = claimable_by_customer_id,
           claimed_at = NOW(),
           claimable_by_customer_id = NULL
     WHERE id IN (...)
       AND claimable_by_customer_id = ?
       AND claimed_at IS NULL;

  All updates in one transaction. The WHERE clauses guard
  against a race where two requests try to claim the same
  order.

  Flash: "N orders linked to your account."
  Redirect to /account/orders (which will now include them).

### Dismiss action

POST /account/claims/dismiss

  Sets a session flag to hide the dashboard notification
  for this session. Does not modify the orders.

  The orders remain claimable. If the customer visits
  /account/claims directly, they can still claim them.

## Edge Cases

**Typo email.** Guest types alice@x.com by mistake, meant
alice@y.com. Order becomes claimable by Alice's account.
Alice logs in, sees "1 order matching your email," clicks
Review. She sees the order - the shipping city is unknown
to her, the items are foreign. She clicks "Leave as guest
orders." The order stays orphaned. Correct outcome.

**Customer deletion between order and claim.** The FK
ON DELETE SET NULL clears claimable_by_customer_id when
the customer is deleted. The order becomes a normal guest
order. No crash.

**Customer email change after order.** The customer changes
their email on /account/password (or wherever). The order's
guest_email still shows the old address. claimable_by_customer_id
still points at the customer. The customer still sees the
claim. That is correct - the order WAS theirs, and the email
matched at order time.

**Order placed with toggle off, toggle turned on later.**
The existing orders have no claimable_by_customer_id. They
stay as pure guest orders. New orders get the treatment.
This is the "new orders only" scope decision.

**Two customers with the same email.** Impossible - the
unique key on (store_id, email) prevents it. findByEmail
returns at most one match.
