## Worked on

### The cart and checkout state survival fix

The customer noticed the cart emptied after an order
was placed, even when the payment was abandoned at
Stripe. The order row existed as `unpaid`, but the
cart was gone and the checkout wizard state was gone.
The customer could not retry without re-adding items.

Root cause: two early clears.

  - CheckoutController::place() called
    $session->forget(self::SESSION_KEY) before the
    charge was dispatched. The wizard state was gone
    before Stripe's session was even created.

  - CheckoutService::placeOrder() called
    $this->cart->clear() after the order row
    committed. The cart was gone before the customer
    reached Stripe's page.

Both moved to CheckoutController::confirmation().
The cart and the state now survive the redirect to
the payment provider and the return through the
return controller. They clear when the customer has
seen the confirmation page.

The confirmation clear is guarded by
$session->has(self::SESSION_KEY). A customer
revisiting an old confirmation page does not touch a
cart they might be actively using.

This matches the industry standard. WooCommerce,
Shopify, and every mature platform clear on
confirmed, not on created.

Verified in the browser: an order placed with Stripe
and abandoned at checkout.stripe.com leaves the cart
intact. Returning to /cart shows the items.

### The single-notification mark-read fix

The bell dropdown rendered each notification as a
<a href>. The mark-read endpoint is POST only. A GET
never reached it. Clicking a notification navigated
the customer to the target page but left the
notification unread. The badge only decremented when
the customer opened the message center and clicked
"mark all as read".

The dropdown item is now a POST form. The endpoint
receives the notification id and a redirect_to,
marks the row read, and sends the customer to the
notification's own target.

  - header.php: the <a> is now a <button> inside a
    <form method="POST">. The form carries csrf_field,
    the notification_id, and a redirect_to set to the
    message link.

  - MessageController::markRead() accepts an optional
    redirect_to. Same-origin only: a leading slash is
    required and a leading double slash is rejected.
    The messages page's own mark-read form does not
    send one, so it falls back to the messages index
    on the current tab.

  - storefront.css: the button inherits the anchor's
    visual treatment. Transparent background, no
    border, full width, text-align left.

## Decisions made

**Clear the cart on confirmed, not on created.**
Industry standard. The customer who abandons at a
payment provider and returns later can retry. The
trade-off is unpaid orders accumulating in the
admin. That is a known cost. The mitigation is a
future cron that cancels unpaid orders older than N
hours. Logged in ROADMAP.

**The redirect_to parameter on markRead is
same-origin only.** A leading slash is required. A
leading double slash is rejected. This prevents an
open-redirect attack. The check is small but the
cost of omitting it is a genuine vulnerability.

**The bell dropdown items are POST forms, not GET
links.** The endpoint has always been POST. The
mismatch was the bug. A future pass could add AJAX
and update the badge live without a page load, but
the redirect-based flow works and is the same
pattern the messages page already uses.
